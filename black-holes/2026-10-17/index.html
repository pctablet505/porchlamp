Stars don't review pull requests

The state of open-source black holes: how often a stranger's pull request goes unanswered, in the Porchlamp snapshot of 17 October 2026.

You send a small fix to a project you use every day. Two weeks later, nobody has replied. At more than 1 in 6 active open-source projects, that is the normal experience.

Key findings

More than 1 in 6. In Porchlamp's 2026-10-17 snapshot, 720 of 3,613 active open-source projects (19.9%) left most outside pull requests with no human reply, review or merge within 14 days. #

More stars, more silence. The share rises with stars: 16% of projects with 1,000 to 10,000 stars, 25% at 10,000 to 50,000, and 28% at 50,000 or more. #

Fame is no guarantee. 18 of the 100 most-starred projects are black holes, and only 9 of them reach Porchlamp's top 100. #

Stars barely predict an answer. Across 4,322 ranked projects, the rank correlation between stars and the Porchlamp score is 0.09. #

Most of those pull requests waited. Inside the black-hole projects, 38,671 of 49,188 counted outside pull requests got no human reply within 14 days. #

Do popular projects answer fewer pull requests?

They answer less often, on average. The black-hole share climbs at every step up in stars.

One likely reason: review time stays roughly fixed while attention grows, so the queue grows faster than volunteers can clear it. The data shows the rise, not its cause.

Which package ecosystems answer outside pull requests?

PyPI and npm projects go quiet more often than Rust, Go, Java or systems projects. Each share is computed within its own ecosystem.

How we measured

  • We followed 615,200 pull requests from 148,801 outside contributors to 11,000 GitHub projects, from 20 April to 17 October 2026.
  • A project is judged only when it is licensed, maintained software with at least 10 counted outside pull requests.
  • Another 604 licensed, maintained projects with at least 10 outside pull requests on record are not judged yet: those records were fetched before drafts and closes were recorded, and the rules need both.
  • It is a black hole when, with 95% confidence, more than half of those pull requests got no substantive reply, review or merge within 14 days.
  • Bots, drafts, pull requests their authors closed, brand-new accounts and the project's own members are not counted.
  • Every parameter, gate and weight: Porchlamp methodology.

What this is not

  • Not a verdict on the people who maintain these projects. Review time is scarce, and popular projects receive far more pull requests than anyone can read.
  • GitHub only. Projects that review on Gerrit or a mailing list are left undecided, not failed.
  • One six-month window. The numbers will change at the next snapshot.
  • This page names no project. Each project's record, with the pull requests behind it, is on the Porchlamp rankings.
