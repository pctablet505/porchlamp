Porchlamp rankings

Find the projects that leave the light on.

A porch lamp left on tells a visitor they are expected. Porchlamp ranks open-source projects the same way: by what happens to a stranger's pull request, not by how many stars the project has.

Stars don't review pull requests

  • More than 1 in 4 active projects are black holes. Of the 4,297 licensed, maintained software projects that received at least ten outside pull requests in six months, 1,133 (26.4%) left most of them with no human reply, review or merge for two weeks.
  • More stars, more silence. The black-hole share rises with popularity: 21% of projects with 1k–10k stars, 31% at 10k–50k, and 39% at 50k or more, nearly 4 in 10 of the most-starred.
  • Stars barely predict the ranking. Across the 3,833 ranked projects the rank correlation between stars and the Porchlamp score is 0.13, and 9 of the 100 most-starred projects make the top 100.

Where the numbers come from

We followed 212,390 pull requests from 110,605 outside contributors to 11,000 projects over six months across npm (JavaScript/TypeScript), PyPI (Python), crates.io (Rust), Go, Maven (Java), and systems software: who got an answer, how fast, how many reached a decision, whether first-time contributors came back, and how much other software depends on each project. Black holes, and projects that are archived, unmaintained, unlicensed or not software, are listed but never ranked. Every number traces back to a recorded GitHub response and its content hash (by Rahul Kumar, pctablet505).

18 parameters across 5 pillars and 5 contributor personas

  • Responsiveness (25%): Time to First Response (TTFHR), Decision Rate (90d), First-timer Merge Rate, PR Ghost Rate (14d), and Time to Merge (p90).
  • Criticality (25%): Deduplicated Dependents, OpenSSF Criticality Score, and Monthly Registry Downloads.
  • Governance (20%): Legal IP Regime (OSI license & CLA/DCO), Elephant Factor, Promotion Ladder, and Bus Factor.
  • Rigor (15%): OpenSSF Scorecard Pipeline Defenses, AI Policy Posture, and Substantive Review Rate.
  • Accessibility (15%): Active Good First Issues, Newcomer Conversion, Newcomer Return Rate (90d), and Developer Experience.
